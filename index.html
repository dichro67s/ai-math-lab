AI × Mathematics

SNU AI Mathematics Lab

We build AI systems that read, formalize and check mathematics. Our main focus is autoformalization: translating mathematics written by people into Lean 4, a language in which a computer can verify every step, and measuring rigorously whether the translation says what the mathematician meant.

We build the benchmarks, checkers and leaderboards this needs, and study the systems that solve, generate and verify mathematics.

About

Research at the intersection of AI and mathematics

SNU AI Mathematics Lab studies how machines can formalize, solve, generate and verify mathematical knowledge.

Lab overview

Large language models can now write Lean code that compiles, but a statement that compiles can still say the wrong thing: a hypothesis dropped, a conclusion weakened, a quantity assumed instead of computed. Our work makes that gap measurable. We write benchmark problems from graduate textbooks and recent research papers, build hidden checkers that Lean itself verifies, run public evaluations, and develop methods to judge whether a formalization is faithful when no reference exists.

Research

Main research directions

Six directions, connected by one question: how do we know an AI system got the mathematics right?

01

Autoformalization

Translating textbook and research mathematics into Lean 4 statements and proofs, from graduate algebra, analysis, topology and algebraic geometry to recent arXiv papers.

02

Benchmarks & Leaderboards

ShadowBench, the larger in-house ABM collection, and LIFT@Seoul, our sandboxed evaluation server, with a public leaderboard.

03

Semantic Alignment & Verification

Deciding whether a formal statement means what the informal one says: hidden shadow checks in both directions, and judging alignment when no reference formalization exists.

04

Automated Problem Solving

Studying agentic systems that write, compile and repair Lean in a loop, and where they still fail to state the intended theorem.

05

Automated Problem Generation

Turning papers and textbooks into benchmark problems with reference Lean files and checkers, drafted by models and reviewed by mathematicians.

06

Theorem Generation

Exploring models that propose meaningful conjectures, lemmas and theorems in forms a proof assistant can check.
